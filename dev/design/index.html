Design notes and measurements

This page gives the reasons behind the internal design of the walks and the layouts, and the measurements that support them. Each figure names the Julia version and the script it was taken with. A figure is the first-call time of a cold process, which is compilation and a negligible run, unless the row says otherwise. scripts/wide_branch_cost.jl and scripts/leaf_layout_cost.jl run each row in a process of its own, because compilation is shared across a session and a second row would measure less than the first.

The widest branch that a real consumer has is a flat set of 369 leaves: the parameter set of the MNIST transformer in GMLDatasets.jl.

The walk across the children of a branch

A walk down the tree is dispatch on NetworkParameters, NamedTuple, Tuple or a leaf. A walk across the children of one branch is one call of a primitive of src/unrolled.jl: a map, a left fold, and a map that carries a state from child to child. Each primitive is a generated body that reads child i with getfield at the literal index i. A foreach is the map, called for its effect. A walk binds its own function with the internal _FixFirst and not with Base.Fix1, because a primitive passes one child of each set, and Base.Fix1 takes only one further argument on Julia 1.11.

Why not a Base.tail chain. A chain makes a new tuple type at every level, so a branch of k children costs k specialisations whose argument types are each O(k) long. The 0.2.2 entry of CHANGELOG.md gives the cold first-call times of the chain of NNP 0.2.1 against the written-out walks, on Julia 1.11.9 and 1.13, from scripts/wide_branch_cost.jl. Without @inline the chain is slower still, and it allocates, because the chain is what kept the per-leaf copyto! statically dispatched.

Why not a loop over keys. A loop indexes a heterogeneous tuple at a runtime index, which is not type stable, and costs a dynamic dispatch and an allocation per child.

Why not values of the branch. values materialises a temporary tuple per branch and per set, so mapparameters!(f, dest, src) allocates on every call that way. The primitives read every set in place.

A fold reached through a caller that does not specialise. A 369-leaf fold reached through a @noinline caller that does not annotate op::F where {F} allocates, at arity one and at arity two, and allocates nothing with the annotation. test/integration/wide_branches.jl asserts both sides. The walks of this package do not annotate op, because the boxing is the caller's.

The Base.tail folds of GeometricOptimizers. Three such folds (l2norm, solution_scale and _dot) took much longer to compile at 369 children on Julia 1.12 and 1.13 than on 1.11. The tailfold column of scripts/wide_branch_cost.jl reproduces the effect: on a branch of 369 children a chain takes 1.94 s on 1.11.9, 28.55 s on 1.12.7 and 37.86 s on 1.13.0-rc3, where the written-out fold takes 2.11, 0.80 and 0.47 s.

Two walks that take another form

mapparameters and mapstorage hand a branch wider than 32 children to Base.map. They run once per cache or per device move, not per iteration, and their compile time is paid per pair of function and branch shape. On a flat set of 369, map(zero, ps) compiled in 0.01 s and a written-out mapparameters(zero, ps) in 1.51 s. Julia 1.11.9, scripts/wide_branch_cost.jl. GeometricOptimizers reaches six such walks while it builds one OptimizerCache, so a written-out walk raises the compile time of that cache. Base.map past 32 fields drops to its Any32 loop: its result type is not inferred, and it allocates. A closure passed to it costs about as much as a plain function. 32 is the width to which Base unrolls a tuple itself.

The layout carries the offset from child to child. _layout of a branch is the map with a state: each child is laid out after the offset of the one before it, and gives the offset after itself. Two other forms do not work:

  • A left fold whose accumulator holds the children laid out so far gives one tuple type per child, each longer than the last, so inference pays for a type that grows with every child.
  • The lengths of the children first, then each child laid out at its own offset, passes the offsets as a tuple of Ints beside the branch. For a structured leaf whose storage has more blocks than its branch has children, that tuple grows from one level of the recursion to the next, and inference widens the layout type ("failed to optimize due to recursion" in test/quality/jet.jl).

The layout type

LeafLayout keeps no prototype, so a layout type does not carry the array type of each leaf. With the prototype as a type parameter, parameterlayout on a flat set of 369 leaves took 1.35 s as a bare NamedTuple and 13.40 s inside a NetworkParameters; at 768 leaves 2.77 s against 87.77 s. The type tree of the wrapped 369-leaf layout had 1 849 nodes, against 742 without the parameter. Without it the two shapes cost the same: 1.05 s wrapped against 1.04 s bare at 369, 3.01 s against 3.07 s at 768, and 0.84 s against 0.85 s on a 16 × 24 set of 384 leaves. Julia 1.11, 1.12 and 1.13 agree: 1.05, 1.13 and 1.03 s at 369 wrapped. The whole 369-wrapped path of parameterlayout, flatten and unflatten took 21.90 s with the parameter and 8.64 s without it. Julia 1.11 where no other version is named; NNP 0.2.2 for the figures with the prototype and NNP 0.2.3 for those without; scripts/leaf_layout_cost.jl and scripts/wide_branch_cost.jl, whose shared row agrees to 0.1 s.

A leaf is terminal or wrapped by dispatch on the type of its storage. An === test in one method body gave each leaf layout a three-way union, which inference then carries through every child of a branch. A leaf whose freeparameters returns a distinct object of its own type is terminal by this rule; such a leaf has no working layout either way, since the descent into its storage does not end.
