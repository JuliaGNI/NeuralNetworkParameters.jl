Design notes and measurements

This page gives the reasons behind the internal design of the walks and the layouts, and the measurements that support them. Each figure names the Julia version and the script it was taken with. A figure is the first-call time of a cold process, which is compilation and a negligible run, unless the row says otherwise. scripts/wide_branch_cost.jl and scripts/leaf_layout_cost.jl run each row in a process of its own, because compilation is shared across a session and a second row would measure less than the first.

The widest branch that a real consumer has is a flat set of 369 leaves: the parameter set of the MNIST transformer in GMLDatasets.jl.

The walk across the children of a branch

The layout and the flattening walk down the tree by dispatch on NetworkParameters, NamedTuple, Tuple or a leaf. They walk across the children of one branch with one call of a primitive of src/unrolled.jl: a map, a left fold, and a map that carries a state from child to child. Each primitive is a generated body that reads child i with getfield at the literal index i. A foreach is the map, called for its effect. Such a walk binds its own function with Base.Fix1, and a primitive passes it one child of each set. The maps, the in-place walks and the folds take another form, described in the next section.

Why not a Base.tail chain. A chain makes a new tuple type at every level, so a branch of k children costs k specialisations whose argument types are each O(k) long. The 0.2.2 entry of CHANGELOG.md gives the cold first-call times of the chain of NNP 0.2.1 against the written-out walks, on Julia 1.11.9 and 1.13, from scripts/wide_branch_cost.jl. Without @inline the chain is slower still, and it allocates, because the chain is what kept the per-leaf copyto! statically dispatched.

Why not a loop over keys. A loop indexes a heterogeneous tuple at a runtime index, which is not type stable, and costs a dynamic dispatch and an allocation per child.

Why not values of the branch. values materialises a temporary tuple per branch and per set, so mapparameters!(f, dest, src) allocates on every call that way. The primitives read every set in place.

A fold reached through a caller that does not specialise. A 369-leaf fold reached through a @noinline caller that does not annotate op::F where {F} allocates, at arity one and at arity two, and allocates nothing with the annotation. test/integration/wide_branches.jl asserts both sides. The walks of this package do not annotate op, because the boxing is the caller's.

The Base.tail folds of GeometricOptimizers. Three such folds (l2norm, solution_scale and _dot) took much longer to compile at 369 children on Julia 1.12 and 1.13 than on 1.11. The tailfold column of scripts/wide_branch_cost.jl reproduces the effect: on a branch of 369 children a chain takes 1.94 s on 1.11.9, 28.55 s on 1.12.7 and 37.86 s on 1.13.0-rc3, where the written-out fold takes 2.11, 0.80 and 0.47 s.

The maps, the in-place walks and the folds are one body per tree

mapparameters, mapstorage, foreachparameters, mapparameters!, mapstorage!, foldparameters and foldstorage read the whole tree off the types of their arguments when they are generated, and each is one body with one call per leaf. The key and arity checks are the generator's, as in the primitives. The maps check the keys of a branch before the number of its children, so two keyed branches of different widths are reported by their keys; the other walks and the primitives check the number first.

Why not a walk that recurses through its own methods. An optimizer that steps each leaf with its own method runs a walk in the function of another one. A walk that recurses through its own methods then puts the same method on the inference stack twice, the second time with a function argument nested one level deeper, such as Base.Fix1{typeof(foreachparameters), Step{…}}. Inference reads that as recursion that might not end and widens the inner call, so the inner walk dispatches dynamically and boxes its function at every leaf. NNP 0.5.1 walks that way. With an inner walk on a one-leaf set of each of the 3 leaves of a set, its foreachparameters in foreachparameters and mapstorage! in mapstorage! allocate 96 B per step, or 192 and 240 B with five captured Float32 and Float64 coefficients, and each of its folds in itself 144 B. The generated walks allocate nothing in all these cases. Julia 1.13.1, julia --project=. scripts/nested_walk_cost.jl nested.

An optimizer that builds its cache and its state per leaf runs a map in the function of another map, and a map that recurses through its own methods returns a type that inference widens the same way. On NNP 0.5.2, mapparameters whose function runs mapparameters on a set of one leaf, of two leaves, or of one leaf at arity two, does not infer a concrete result type in any of the three cases, in Float32 or Float64; the generated mapparameters infers all six. mapstorage in mapstorage on a set of two leaves does not infer on NNP 0.5.2 either, and infers in both element types as a generated body. Julia 1.13.2, scripts/nested_walk_cost.jl nestedmap. The TrainingOptimizer of a CompositeMethod in GeometricOptimizers is such a caller: on NNP 0.5.2 its type is not inferred, and a caller that builds it and steps in one function allocates 64 B per step.

Why f at a leaf and not the leaf method. A generated body that calls the leaf method of foreachparameters or of foldparameters at each leaf puts that method on the inference stack twice as well, both times called from the same generated method. Where the inner walk takes more sets than the outer one, and at least four, inference shortens the longer argument list of the second call to a Vararg and does not specialise it on its function. The per-leaf Adam step of GeometricOptimizers walks six sets inside a walk of five, and then boxes its function at every leaf, 32 B in Float32 and 48 B in Float64. So these two walks call f or op at a leaf directly, and test/walk.jl runs a walk of four sets inside a walk of two for each. The storage walks call their leaf method, which decides at run time with freeparameters whether a leaf is its own storage, so mapstorage! and foldstorage of four or more sets inside a walk of fewer sets still allocate at every leaf: on the 3 leaves of scripts/nested_walk_cost.jl wide, 192 B and 48 B per step (KNOWN_ISSUES.md K11).

Two walks that take another form

mapparameters and mapstorage hand a branch wider than 32 children to Base.map. They run once per cache or per device move, not per iteration, and their compile time is paid per pair of function and branch shape. On a flat set of 369, map(zero, ps) compiled in 0.01 s and a written-out mapparameters(zero, ps) in 1.51 s. Julia 1.11.9, scripts/wide_branch_cost.jl. The generated mapparameters maps across such a branch with Base.map too: written out in its body, with _WRITE_OUT_MAX_CHILDREN raised above 369, its first call of zero on that set takes 10.4 s, against 0.03 s with Base.map. Julia 1.13.2, two cold processes each. GeometricOptimizers reaches six such walks while it builds one OptimizerCache, so a written-out walk raises the compile time of that cache. Base.map past 32 fields drops to its Any32 loop: its result type is not inferred, and it allocates. A closure passed to it costs about as much as a plain function. 32 is the width to which Base unrolls a tuple itself.

The layout carries the offset from child to child. _layout of a branch is the map with a state: each child is laid out after the offset of the one before it, and gives the offset after itself. Two other forms do not work:

  • A left fold whose accumulator holds the children laid out so far gives one tuple type per child, each longer than the last, so inference pays for a type that grows with every child.
  • The lengths of the children first, then each child laid out at its own offset, passes the offsets as a tuple of Ints beside the branch. For a structured leaf whose storage has more blocks than its branch has children, that tuple grows from one level of the recursion to the next, and inference widens the layout type ("failed to optimize due to recursion" in test/quality/jet.jl).

The layout type

LeafLayout keeps no prototype, so a layout type does not carry the array type of each leaf. With the prototype as a type parameter, parameterlayout on a flat set of 369 leaves took 1.35 s as a bare NamedTuple and 13.40 s inside a NetworkParameters; at 768 leaves 2.77 s against 87.77 s. The type tree of the wrapped 369-leaf layout had 1 849 nodes, against 742 without the parameter. Without it the two shapes cost the same: 1.05 s wrapped against 1.04 s bare at 369, 3.01 s against 3.07 s at 768, and 0.84 s against 0.85 s on a 16 × 24 set of 384 leaves. Julia 1.11, 1.12 and 1.13 agree: 1.05, 1.13 and 1.03 s at 369 wrapped. The whole 369-wrapped path of parameterlayout, flatten and unflatten took 21.90 s with the parameter and 8.64 s without it. Julia 1.11 where no other version is named; NNP 0.2.2 for the figures with the prototype and NNP 0.2.3 for those without; scripts/leaf_layout_cost.jl and scripts/wide_branch_cost.jl, whose shared row agrees to 0.1 s.

A leaf is terminal or wrapped by dispatch on the type of its storage. An === test in one method body gave each leaf layout a three-way union, which inference then carries through every child of a branch. A leaf whose freeparameters returns a distinct object of its own type is terminal by this rule; such a leaf has no working layout either way, since the descent into its storage does not end.
